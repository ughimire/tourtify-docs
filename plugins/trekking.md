# Trekking & expeditions <Badge type="tip" text="Growth+" />

Trekking & expeditions gives multi-day trekking operators the tools to run treks properly: permits and fees per traveller worked out from their nationality, document checks, the permit checklist and agency submission sheet, crew with licences, porter loads and wages, teahouse nights, an emergency plan for the crew, daily check-ins from the trail, and an incident and evacuation log.

::: info At a glance
- **Plan:** Growth and Scale
- **Category:** Operations
- **Where:** Plugins › Trekking & expeditions, and Operations › Trekking
- **Needs:** [Traveller details](./traveller-forms), which collects passports, nationality and insurance
:::

## How it works

1. **Set up a tour as a trek.** Add the permits it needs, mark rest days and high points, choose the usual lodges, and write its emergency plan and crew kit checklist.
2. **Travellers book as usual.** Their nationality, passport and other documents are collected with [Traveller details](./traveller-forms). Permit fees are worked out for each traveller.
3. **Before departure,** the departure page shows which permits are issued, which documents are complete and which lodges are confirmed. Put crew on the departure and check their licence and insurance dates.
4. **On the trail,** the crew checks in from their phone page, which also shows the emergency plan. If no check-in arrives in time, your team gets an email.
5. **After the trek,** crew pay is worked out and you record payments.

## Turn it on

1. Go to **Plugins** in the console.
2. Find **Trekking & expeditions** and flip its switch on. Traveller details is switched on with it.
3. Select **Configure** to set your country, document, crew and safety rules, then **Save**.
4. Open **Operations › Trekking** and choose **Set up your first trek**.

## Settings

**Permits**

| Setting | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Country your treks run in | Two-letter country code. Its citizens pay the national fee; SAARC rules apply when it is a SAARC country. | NP |
| Exchange rates for permit fees | When fees are in another currency than a booking, e.g. "NPR=136.5" means 1 of your main currency = 136.5 NPR. Rates from the Currency switcher are used first. | Empty |

**Documents**

| Setting | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Passport must be valid for (months after the trek) | Passports that expire sooner are flagged. | 6 |

**Crew**

| Setting | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Most trekkers per licensed guide | Nepal's restricted-area rule (2026) is at most 7 trekkers per guide. | 7 |
| Trekkers per porter | Used to warn when a departure has too few porters. | 2 |
| Maximum porter load (kg) | Porter welfare guidelines (IPPG) set 30 kg as the upper limit in Nepal; many responsible operators use 20–25 kg. | 25 |

**Safety**

| Setting | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Alert when the crew hasn't checked in for (hours) | During a trek, your team gets an email when no check-in arrives in this time. Set 0 to turn the alert off. | 26 |

## Use the trekking pages

**Operations › Trekking** lists trek departures (**Upcoming & on trek** or **Past**) with travellers, permits issued, crew, lodges and the last check-in. The top shows **On the trail now**, **Permits still open**, **Open incidents** and **Active crew**.

**Permits**

Your permit catalogue: national parks, conservation areas, restricted areas and trekking cards, with fees per nationality group. **Add Nepal permits** starts you off with Nepal's common permits (Sagarmatha, ACAP, MCAP, Langtang, TIMS and the restricted areas) and their published fees; check each fee before you rely on it. Or select **New permit** and set the type, region, issuing office, fees (per person, per day, per week, or first days then daily, with seasons), documents needed, how many days ahead to apply, the smallest group, and whether **Only a licensed agency can apply** or **A licensed guide must go with the group**.

**Trek setup** (per tour)

- **Permits**: which permits the trek needs, from which day and for how many days, and who pays: **Included in the price** or **Traveller pays**.
- **Day by day**: from the tour's itinerary. Mark acclimatisation or rest days, the day's high point and the usual lodge, with crew notes.
- **Emergency plan**: emergency contacts, helicopter landing points, hospitals and aid posts, and instructions for the crew. It's printed in the crew pack and shown on the crew's phone page.
- **Crew kit checklist**: copied to each departure, where the crew leader ticks it off.

**A trek departure**

Tabs for **Permits**, **Crew**, **Lodges**, **Safety** and **Plan & kit**, an **Altitude profile**, the **Manifest** and the **Crew pack**. Record the agency's permit **Application number** with the issuing office.

**Crew**

Everyone who works your treks: guides, porters, cooks and Sherpas, with licence number and expiry, insurer and policy, daily wage and daily allowance, and the most each porter carries. Licence and insurance dates are checked before each departure.

**Crew pay**

Days × daily wage and allowance, plus agreed extras, for every trek. **Owed now (treks finished)** lists who to pay; select **Pay** to record a payment (cash, bank transfer, mobile wallet, cheque or other). **Undo payment** makes the treks owed again.

## Good to know

- Fees are worked out per traveller from their nationality and the days the trek spends in the area. SAARC travellers pay the foreigners' fee when there is no SAARC fee. Citizens with no fee don't need the permit.
- "Traveller pays" permits are added to the booking when you charge them from the departure.
- Permit fees and rules change. Check each fee and note when you last checked it in the permit.
- Turning the plugin off: permits, crew and safety tools leave the console. Treks, permits and crew records are kept, and Traveller details stays on.
